European mission captures interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS ejecting 175 million liters of water daily

The European Space Agency (ESA) has reported that the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS is currently ejecting approximately 175 million liters of water daily. This significant discovery was made during the comet's close approach to the Sun, which occurred in July 2026.

This finding is important as it offers insights into the composition and behavior of interstellar objects. The ESA plans to continue monitoring 3I/ATLAS to gather more data on its water output and other characteristics.

Key facts

  • Comet: 3I/ATLAS
  • Water ejection rate: 175 million liters per day
  • Close approach to the Sun: July 2026
  • Agency involved: European Space Agency (ESA)

Timeline

3I/ATLAS was first detected in 2022 as it entered the Solar System. Following its trajectory, scientists observed its approach to the Sun, which peaked in July 2026.

During this period, the ESA conducted detailed observations and confirmed the comet's high water ejection rate.

Official statements

“The data collected from 3I/ATLAS will enhance our understanding of interstellar comets and their role in the Solar System,” said a spokesperson from the ESA.

Context

3I/ATLAS is one of the few documented interstellar comets. Its discovery adds to a growing body of research on celestial objects that originate from outside our Solar System, providing valuable information about their properties and behaviors.

What’s next

The ESA will continue its observations of 3I/ATLAS to study its water output and potential implications for understanding the origins of water in the Solar System.
