What Is an Interstellar Object? How Scientists Know

Published 2026-08-16 13:05 UTC

Comet 3I/ATLAS Identified as Third Known Interstellar Object

NASA has confirmed that Comet 3I/ATLAS is the third known interstellar object to pass through the Solar System, following its discovery by the ATLAS survey in July 2025. The comet's unique hyperbolic orbit indicates that it is not bound to the Sun and will continue on its trajectory into interstellar space.

This discovery matters as it provides scientists with insights into the formation and composition of celestial bodies from other star systems. The identification of 3I/ATLAS helps to expand our understanding of the interstellar population and guides future observational efforts to detect more such objects.

Key facts

  • 3I/ATLAS was first reported in July 2025.
  • It is the third interstellar object confirmed by NASA, following 1I/‘Oumuamua in 2017 and 2I/Borisov in 2019.
  • The “I” in the designation stands for interstellar, with the number indicating the order of confirmation.
  • NASA's James Webb Space Telescope detected a unique chemical composition in 3I/ATLAS.
  • The comet features a high carbon-dioxide-to-water ratio and methane in its infrared observations.

Timeline

In July 2025, the ATLAS survey reported the discovery of Comet 3I/ATLAS. Subsequent analysis revealed older observations in telescope archives, extending the observed arc and improving the orbit calculation. This tracking is crucial for distinguishing a true interstellar path from potential measurement errors.

The comet's hyperbolic orbit has been confirmed through repeated position measurements, allowing astronomers to understand its trajectory and speed. NASA has employed spectroscopy to analyze the comet's composition, further indicating its interstellar origin.

Official statements

“The unique chemical mix detected in 3I/ATLAS reflects the different environment in which the comet formed,” said a NASA representative. “This discovery enhances our knowledge of interstellar objects and provides a glimpse into the conditions beyond our Solar System.”

Context

Comet 3I/ATLAS is part of a growing list of interstellar objects that have been observed. The previous two, 1I/‘Oumuamua and 2I/Borisov, also provided valuable data that helps scientists understand the diversity of bodies that exist beyond our Solar System. As more interstellar objects are detected, researchers hope to uncover trends in their composition and behavior.

What’s next

Future observational efforts will focus on identifying additional interstellar objects. The detection of more comets like 3I/ATLAS could provide crucial information about the nature of interstellar materials and the environments from which they originate.
