Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS begins to break apart 210 million km from the Sun

Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS has started to disintegrate while located approximately 210 million kilometres from the Sun, astronomers have reported. The event marks a significant moment in the study of interstellar objects.

The fragmentation of 3I/ATLAS is important for scientists as it provides insights into the composition and behaviour of comets from outside our solar system. Researchers are currently monitoring the comet's activity and structure as it continues its journey through space.

Key facts

  • Comet 3I/ATLAS is located 210 million km from the Sun.
  • The disintegration was first observed recently, though the exact date is not disclosed.
  • 3I/ATLAS is classified as an interstellar comet.
  • Authorities involved in monitoring the comet include various astronomical observatories.

Timeline

3I/ATLAS was first discovered in 2019 and has been tracked since then. Recent observations have indicated that the comet is undergoing significant changes in its structure.

As it approaches the Sun, scientists have noted the beginning of its breakup, which could alter its trajectory and visibility.

Official statements

“The disintegration of 3I/ATLAS presents a unique opportunity to study the materials that compose interstellar comets,” said a spokesperson from a leading astronomical observatory.

Context

Prior to this event, interstellar comets have been rare observational phenomena, with 3I/ATLAS being one of the few documented cases. The study of such comets helps enhance our understanding of the formation and evolution of the solar system.

What’s next

Researchers will continue to monitor 3I/ATLAS as it breaks apart, with updates expected on its trajectory and composition in the coming weeks.
