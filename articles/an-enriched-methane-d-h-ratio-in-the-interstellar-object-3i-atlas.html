An Enriched Methane D/H Ratio in the Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS

Published 2026-08-19 16:55 UTC

Enriched Methane D/H Ratio Detected in Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS

Researchers have found an unexpectedly high deuterium-to-hydrogen (D/H) ratio in the methane of the interstellar object 3I/ATLAS, according to a study published on August 18, 2026. The analysis was conducted using near-infrared spectra from the James Webb Space Telescope.

This discovery is significant as it provides insights into the formation of planetary systems and the chemical processes occurring in interstellar environments. The elevated D/H ratio could offer clues about the conditions under which the ices of 3I/ATLAS formed, indicating a cold, low metallicity environment with high cosmic ray irradiation.

Key facts

  • Object: 3I/ATLAS
  • Measurement Date: August 18, 2026
  • Research Team: Nathan X. Roth, Martin Cordiner, and others
  • D/H Ratio for Methane: (3.33±0.31)%
  • Comparison: 14±2 times higher than that of comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko
  • Instrument Used: James Webb Space Telescope

Timeline

In March 2026, researchers began collecting near-infrared spectra of 3I/ATLAS using the James Webb Space Telescope. By August 18, 2026, they had analyzed the data and reported their findings regarding the D/H ratio in methane.

Official statements

“The D/H ratio in 3I/ATLAS exceeds any other value for methane measured in the solar system,” said Nathan X. Roth, a lead researcher on the study.
“This detection of deuterated organic molecules is exceedingly rare and expands our understanding of interstellar chemistry,” added Martin Cordiner, co-author of the study.

Context

The detection of a high D/H ratio in 3I/ATLAS is consistent with trends observed in other celestial bodies. Previous studies have indicated that deuteration in organics often surpasses that of water, with variations observed in different environments across the solar system and the nearby interstellar medium.

What’s next

Researchers plan to continue their investigations into the chemical composition of 3I/ATLAS and other interstellar objects to further understand the implications of these findings for planetary formation and evolution.
